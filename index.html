Features

Everything a modern vault should do, without a subscription.

Zero-knowledge vault

Ten item types: logins, cards, identities, Wi-Fi, SSH keys, API keys, licences, notes, passkeys and custom. Each is encrypted separately before it touches storage.

Biometric unlock

The vault key is wrapped by a hardware-backed Android Keystore key that needs your fingerprint or face, and is destroyed if your biometrics change.

Encrypted sharing

Send a one-off copy, or share live with view or edit access. Removing someone rotates the item key, so they can't read what changes next.

Built-in 2FA codes

TOTP codes (RFC 6238) live next to the login they protect. Add them from a setup key, an otpauth:// link or by scanning the QR code.

Autofill and passkeys

Fills matching logins in apps and websites, offers to save new ones, and acts as a passkey provider on Android 14+ through Credential Manager.

Security centre

A health score plus weak, reused, old and duplicate password checks, all computed on the device. Breach checks use k-anonymity, so only 5 hash characters ever leave the phone.

Google Drive backup

Optional, off by default. Encrypted backups go to a hidden app-only folder in your own Drive. The latest 5 are kept. Google can't read them.

Recovery without a backdoor

An optional offline recovery key can reset a forgotten master password. The server keeps only a copy of your vault key locked by that recovery key.

Local-first sync

Works fully offline. Changes sync in the background with revision checks; when two devices edit the same item, both versions are kept.

Move in from anywhere

CSV import from Chrome, Firefox, Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass and Dashlane. Encrypted .synapse backups for moving between phones.

Material You, English and Hindi

Light and dark themes, wallpaper colours or six hand-tuned accents, and a full Hindi translation.

Private alerts

New sign-in, share and sync notifications over FCM. They carry event types and IDs only, never anything from your vault.

Security model

One password in. Two keys out. One of them never leaves your phone.

  1. 1

    You type your master password

    It is never sent anywhere and never stored, not even as a hash.

  2. 2

    Argon2id stretches it

    64 MiB of memory, 3 passes, a random 16-byte salt. Memory-hard on purpose: GPU and ASIC cracking rigs lose most of their advantage.

  3. 3

    HKDF-SHA256 splits it in two

    An authKey proves who you are and is hashed again with Argon2id on the server. A KEK stays on the device. Knowing one reveals nothing about the other.

  4. 4

    The KEK unwraps a random 256-bit vault key

    So changing your master password re-wraps one key instead of re-encrypting everything.

  5. 5

    Every item is sealed on its own

    AES-256-GCM with a fresh 96-bit nonce, and the item ID bound in as associated data, so ciphertexts can't be swapped between items.

key-hierarchy.txt
# on the device
mk      = Argon2id(password, salt,
            m=64MiB, t=3, p=1)
authKey = HKDF(mk, "synapse/auth/v1")
kek     = HKDF(mk, "synapse/kek/v1")

vaultKey = random(32)
wrapped  = AES-GCM(kek, vaultKey)

item = 0x01 ‖ nonce ‖
       AES-GCM(vaultKey, json,
         aad="synapse/item/v1/{id}")

# sent to the server
authKey  → stored as Argon2id(authKey)
wrapped, item ciphertexts → stored as-is

FAQ

Questions people ask

What if I forget my master password?+

If you set up a recovery kit, you can reset it with your recovery key. Without one, nobody can open your vault, including us. That's what zero-knowledge means. Set up the recovery kit in Settings → Account.

Why sign in with Google or GitHub if there's a master password?+

They identify your account for sync and sharing. The master password is a second, separate layer that encrypts the vault. Someone who takes over your Google account still can't decrypt anything.

Does it work offline?+

Yes. The encrypted vault lives on your phone and unlocks on-device. Changes sync when you're back online.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?+

It's coming. Until then the APK is signed with the Synapse release key, and each release lists its SHA-256 so you can verify the download.

Can I move my passwords from another manager?+

Yes. Export a CSV from Chrome, Firefox, Bitwarden, 1Password, LastPass or Dashlane and import it in Settings → Backup & transfer. Delete the CSV afterwards; it isn't encrypted.

How do I delete my account?+

Settings → Account → Delete account, confirmed with your master password. It removes your account and encrypted vault from the server and the phone, and can delete your Drive backups too.
